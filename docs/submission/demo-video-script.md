# GatherGraph demo video

- Current narrated cut: **1 minute 24 seconds**
- Hard limit: **under 3 minutes**
- Format: public YouTube video with spoken audio; no unlicensed music or third-party footage

## Narrative

### 0:00–0:12 — The problem

**Picture:** GatherGraph hero and the example request.

**Voice:** “You describe the catered event once, then stay in your chat. GatherGraph lets your agent coordinate the venue, caterer, and setup across separate websites without asking you to search, compare, or copy the details yourself.”

### 0:12–0:27 — Why WebMCP matters

**Picture:** Side-by-side comparison, then the three provider plan card.

**Voice:** “The venue, catering, and caterer-access services are independent same-origin documents. Each owns three WebMCP tools. GatherGraph owns four more for composition, conflict repair, approval, and the final simulated dossier.”

**On-screen proof:** Briefly show native mode and the ownership split: four
parent orchestration tools plus three tools in each of the venue, food, and
logistics documents—thirteen across four document contexts.

### 0:27–0:37 — One human request

**Picture:** Run the example: “Plan an accessible dinner for 40 people under £1,800.”

**Voice:** “The person describes the outcome, not the clicks. The agent gathers structured capacity, accessibility, menu, allergen, route, timing, and footprint evidence from the right provider.”

**On-screen proof:** Provider strips update; receipts appear.

### 0:37–0:52 — Visible conflict and repair

**Picture:** Workspace showing “Caterer arrival at 16:45 conflicts”; invoke repair.

**Voice:** “The first plan is not silently presented as correct. The caterer proposes arriving at 16:45, but the venue does not open to suppliers until 17:00. The agent moves setup to 17:15 and records exactly what changed.”

**On-screen proof:** Conflict changes to “Caterer arrival moved to 17:15”; ledger receipt appears.

### 0:52–1:07 — Human approval boundary

**Picture:** Request approval, show the dialog, approve, then commit the simulated dossier.

**Voice:** “Only now does the agent come back to the person. One final yes or no is the only required human action. Without approval, dossier creation fails closed. With it, the demo assembles a browser-local dossier—no booking, payment, or message is sent.”

### 1:07–1:24 — Shared control and close

**Picture:** Budget field, provider surfaces, execution ledger.

**Voice:** “The person normally stays in chat and sees the final plan. This optional audit view shows every supplier check and receipt, and lets the person take over manually at any time. GatherGraph: one human brief, every supplier coordinated by an agent, and one final decision that stays with you.”

## Recording checklist

- Record the deployed production build, not the development fallback unless explicitly explaining it.
- Keep browser zoom at 100% and notifications hidden.
- Show native WebMCP discovery before the guided workflow.
- Capture clear pointer movement and wait for each receipt to render.
- Record voice cleanly; do not use copyrighted music.
- Export below 2:50 to leave safety margin under the three-minute limit.
- Review the final YouTube upload in a logged-out window before adding its URL.
